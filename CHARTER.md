# NaijaScamCorpus Founding Charter

**Version:** 0.1 (founding draft)  
**Status:** Proposed for public review  
**Scope:** Africa-wide from inception  
**Governance identity:** Independent open-source community  
**Working name:** NaijaScamCorpus (public-brand review pending)

## 1. Preamble

Digital scams exploit differences in language, trust, payment infrastructure, regulation, and access to reliable information. African communities need research resources that reflect these realities without exposing victims, amplifying false allegations, or creating new opportunities for abuse.

NaijaScamCorpus is established as an independent, community-governed, public-interest initiative to improve the quality, availability, and responsible use of African-context scam-pattern research data.

## 2. Mission

To build and maintain an open, multilingual, evidence-informed and privacy-preserving corpus of scam patterns relevant to African digital communities, enabling better research, education, defensive engineering, and cross-community understanding.

## 3. Vision

An Africa-wide research commons in which researchers, developers, language communities, civil-society organisations, and responsible institutions can collaborate using trustworthy, well-documented and safely shared scam-pattern data.

## 4. Scope

The project is pan-African from day one. Its schema, governance, contribution rules, and taxonomy must not assume that Nigeria is the default or only context.

The project may cover:

- countries and territories in Africa, represented using maintained ISO country identifiers;
- African languages, dialects, transliteration, and code-switching;
- scam and social-engineering patterns across SMS, messaging, email, voice, social platforms, websites, advertisements, and payment journeys;
- impersonation, advance-fee, investment, employment, romance, account-takeover, payment-redirection, mobile-money, delivery, charity, and emerging scam patterns;
- the tactics used, the requested action, the impersonated sector, and defensive guidance;
- research benchmarks and evaluations of detection methods.

Initial geographic coverage will be developed through regional working groups. “Africa-wide from day one” describes the project's governance and data model; it is not a claim that every country or language is already represented.

## 5. Public-interest objectives

1. Establish a consistent, versioned taxonomy for scam patterns in African contexts.
2. Publish high-quality, appropriately licensed, privacy-reviewed research records.
3. Represent language and regional context without stereotyping communities.
4. Enable reproducible research and fair model evaluation.
5. Make contribution possible for non-programmers and technical contributors.
6. Promote practical public education based on reviewed patterns.
7. Support collaboration without becoming an operational fraud-reporting or enforcement body.

## 6. Operating principles

- **People before data:** no research value justifies exposing a victim.
- **Data minimisation:** collect only fields required for the defined research purpose.
- **Provenance:** record where a claim came from and what it does and does not establish.
- **Uncertainty:** distinguish synthetic examples, allegations, reports, corroborated patterns, and verified public advisories.
- **Human review:** automation may assist triage but must not independently approve high-impact claims.
- **Safety:** do not publish material that enables fraud, harassment, targeting, or impersonation.
- **Fair representation:** avoid treating one country, language, ethnicity, or community as representative of the continent.
- **Correction and withdrawal:** provide a documented route to correct, restrict, or remove a record.
- **Open participation:** accept non-code contributions and publish transparent criteria.
- **Independence:** no single company, funder, government, or individual owns the community's decisions.

## 7. What the project will publish

At launch:

- taxonomies and annotation guidelines;
- schemas and validation tools;
- synthetic examples clearly labelled as synthetic;
- literature and public-source references;
- dataset cards and methodology notes;
- reproducible benchmark specifications.

Later, and only after the relevant review gates:

- privacy-reviewed, rights-cleared pattern abstractions based on suitable public sources or properly authorised research;
- aggregate statistics where re-identification risk is acceptably low;
- versioned evaluation datasets with documented provenance and limitations.

## 8. What the project will not publish or operate

- raw reports from victims or unredacted incident evidence;
- phone numbers, email addresses, account identifiers, national identity numbers, payment references, credentials, OTPs, private conversations, or identity documents;
- live clickable phishing links or operational malicious infrastructure in the initial public corpus;
- unverified naming-and-shaming lists or allegations against identifiable persons or organisations;
- private datasets obtained without authority, consent where required, or a lawful basis;
- services that claim to determine whether a person or organisation is a criminal;
- law-enforcement case files or evidence repositories.

## 9. Community and participation

Participation is open to individuals and organisations that agree to the Charter, Code of Conduct, Data Governance Policy, and contribution requirements. Contributors may participate as researchers, language reviewers, annotators, software engineers, educators, privacy reviewers, or community organisers.

Contributions are accepted on merit and safety, not on employer, nationality, seniority, or affiliation. Contributors must not imply that participation grants authority to speak for the project.

## 10. Governance

The project will use transparent, documented community governance. Maintainers are stewards, not owners. A maintainer may not unilaterally approve a sensitive dataset release. The governance model, role descriptions, voting thresholds, conflict-of-interest requirements, and removal process are defined in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).

## 11. Independence and conflicts of interest

The project is independent of any single commercial provider or government body. Members must disclose relevant conflicts, including employment, funding, vendor relationships, research interests, or direct involvement in a matter under review. A conflicted reviewer must recuse themselves from the affected decision.

The project may accept transparent, non-controlling support. Funding sources and material in-kind support must be disclosed. No funder may control dataset inclusion, research conclusions, or correction decisions.

## 12. Licensing and stewardship

Code, documentation, taxonomy, and dataset records may carry different licences. No blanket licence applies to third-party sources. Contributors must have the rights and authority to submit the material they contribute. See [DATA_LICENSE.md](DATA_LICENSE.md).

## 13. Accountability and review

The project will publish:

- release notes and dataset cards;
- material changes to methodology and taxonomy;
- correction and withdrawal notices, subject to safety and privacy;
- governance decisions and conflict disclosures where appropriate;
- annual review of this Charter and the Data Governance Policy.

## 14. Amendments

Any community member may propose an amendment through a public governance proposal. Material amendments require a minimum 14-day comment period, approval by at least two-thirds of active Steering Council members, and affirmative review by the Safety & Data Governance function where privacy or safety is affected.

## 15. Founding status

This Charter establishes the project's initial principles and proposed governance. It does not create a legal entity, confer regulatory authority, or guarantee that every participating member has identical legal obligations. Before collecting non-synthetic data at scale, the community must obtain jurisdiction-specific legal and privacy review and decide whether a formal legal host or fiscal sponsor is required.
